The used vehicle market accounts for 70% of U.S. vehicle purchases, yet consumer preferences for used battery electric vehicles (BEVs) remain poorly understood. A key barrier is information asymmetry around battery condition: unlike mileage or age, battery degradation varies substantially across vehicles of the same model year, making the true condition of a used BEV difficult to assess at the point of sale.
This study uses a discrete choice experiment (DCE) administered to 2,900 U.S. consumers planning to purchase  a used car or SUV within two years. Respondents completed six repeated choice tasks varying powertrain type, driving range, vehicle age, mileage, operating cost, and purchase price across budget-segmented scenarios. A multinomial logit (MNL) model estimates willingness to pay (WTP) for used BEVs relative to comparable conventional vehicles, disaggregated by budget tier, adoption propensity, vehicle type, and vehicle age.
Initial results show that driving range, vehicle age, and adoption propensity are the dominant determinants of WTP. Only likely adopters choosing a 2-year-old, 300-mile-range car show positive WTP — willingness to pay a premium over an equivalent conventional vehicle. For most other segments, used BEVs must be priced at a discount to attract buyers, with required discounts reaching $40,000 for older vehicles among unlikely    adopters. These findings highlight the segments and vehicle characteristics most critical to scaling the used BEV market.
